## Objective

Investigate thermodynamic identifiability in partially observed finite-state continuous-time Markov networks. Given complete infinite-statistics transition-to-transition waiting-time distributions for a subset of observable transitions, characterize the set of microscopic Markov models compatible with those observations and determine whether their steady-state entropy-production rate is unique.

Begin with small finite networks, initially targeting exhaustive or systematic analysis for $N \le 6$, and classify cases in which entropy production is exactly identifiable, bounded but non-unique, or unbounded/non-identifiable. Use numerical counterexample search, symbolic analysis, graph/topology reasoning, and relevant results from stochastic thermodynamics, hidden/aggregated Markov models, phase-type distributions, realization theory, and related mathematics as appropriate.

The goal is a novel, defensible theoretical or computational result suitable for publication. Existing waiting-time entropy estimators, coarse-graining results, hidden-network reconstruction methods, and identifiability theory are prior art to investigate rather than assumptions of novelty. Negative results, impossibility results, counterexamples, or evidence that the proposed formulation is already solved are legitimate research outcomes.
