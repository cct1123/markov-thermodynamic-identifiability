\subsection{Conditioning of the three-state inverse}\label{subsec:conditioning}

Local Lipschitz inversion is a pointwise statement. To quantify its constants,
write $\theta=(r,s,a,c,b,d)$ and let $\phi(\theta)\in\mathbb R^{12}$ collect
the joint transform entries at three distinct positive arguments. With
$K_\lambda=(\lambda I-T)^{-1}$, its full six-rate derivative is
\begin{equation}
 \partial_j\widehat\Psi(\lambda)
 =RK_\lambda(\partial_jT)K_\lambda B
   +RK_\lambda(\partial_jB).\label{eq:transform-jacobian}
\end{equation}
The second term is essential when the observed rates $r,s$ are unknown.
Absolute-rate errors are governed by $J_\theta=D\phi$, whereas relative-rate
errors are governed by $J_{\log}=J_\theta\operatorname{diag}(\theta)$.
These describe different inverse questions. Raw singular values carry time
units; logarithmic coordinates are dimensionless in the fixed time convention.

The rational inverse in Theorem~\ref{thm:inverse} has a derivative that is a
left inverse of $J_\theta$. This remains true at an irreducible tree by
continuity from the positive interior. Consequently the raw derivative has
rank six there. Sparse limits need not make absolute-rate recovery ill
conditioned. For example, at the unit tree and $\lambda=(0.2,1,5)$, its smallest
singular value is approximately $0.00744$.

For the family~\eqref{eq:treefamily}, fix $0<e<1/2$ and let $k\downarrow0$.
The rational formulas also remain nonsingular at this irreducible one-way
limit, although the limiting model itself is outside our reciprocal class.
Thus $J_\theta$ converges to a full-rank matrix while
\begin{equation}
 s_{\min}(J_{\log})=\Theta(k),\qquad
 \operatorname{cond}(J_{\log})=\Theta(k^{-1}).\label{eq:log-conditioning}
\end{equation}
Indeed $s_{\min}(J_\theta D)\ge s_{\min}(J_\theta)\min_jD_{jj}$ for
$D=\operatorname{diag}(\theta)$, and testing the unit $b$ coordinate gives
the matching upper bound. The other five rate coordinates stay positive,
so the limiting nullspace of $J_{\log}$ is exactly the $b=k$ direction.
Its weakest right singular vector converges to that direction, up to sign.
Meanwhile $\partial_b\sig=-J_0(e)/b+O(\log(1/b))$, with
$J_0(e)=e(1-e)/(3+e-e^2)>0$. Relative-rate and entropy sensitivity therefore
diverge even where absolute-rate inversion remains regular.

The choice of Laplace arguments also matters. As three distinct arguments
coalesce, the limiting repeated transform matrix has at most four independent
entries for six rates, so a singular value tends to zero. Arguments all tending
to zero or infinity similarly lose informative variation. Hence ``any three
distinct positive points'' guarantees exact recoverability, with no uniform
conditioning guarantee over designs or sources.

We compared all 165 triples from
$\{0.025,0.05,0.1,0.2,0.5,1,2,5,10,20,40\}$ at the reference in
Fig.~\ref{fig:conditioning}. The largest minimum log singular value occurs
at $(0.2,0.5,5)$. The smallest oracle asymptotic entropy standard error for
the moment estimator below instead occurs at $(0.025,10,20)$: its
$\mathrm{SE}\sqrt n$ is $1.176$, compared with $1.238$ for $(0.2,1,5)$.
The better entropy design has a worse Euclidean condition number.
These are finite-grid comparisons at one reference, not universal optima.
A practical starting point is a separated logarithmic triple spanning a
pilot waiting-time scale, followed by a covariance-aware assessment of the
target uncertainty. Design selection from the same data needs its own
selection correction or an independent pilot.

\subsection{Constructive finite-data inference}\label{subsec:constructive-inference}

The finite-record obstruction does not preclude useful pointwise inference
at a well-resolved interior model. Here we estimate all six rates from the
observed pair alone. Discard the censored interval before the first mark and
record $n$ complete inter-mark intervals. This stops at a random horizon;
it differs from the fixed-window experiment of Corollary~\ref{cor:confidence}.
For incoming mark $I_m$ and outgoing mark $J_m$, the empirical joint moments are
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\mu_{I,jL}
 =\frac1{n_I}\sum_{m:I_m=I}
 e^{-\lambda_j\tau_m}\mathbf1\{J_m=L\},
 \qquad n_I=\sum_m\mathbf1\{I_m=I\}.\label{eq:empirical-laplace}
\end{equation}
We fit all twelve moments by nonlinear least squares over six positive
log-rate coordinates, calculate the stationary entropy of the fitted
generator, and estimate uncertainty by a sandwich delta method.

Successive mark rows are dependent. Nevertheless the centered row features
\begin{equation}
 Z_m^{(I)}=\mathbf1\{I_m=I\}(X_m-\mu_I),\qquad
 X_m=(e^{-\lambda_j\tau_m}\mathbf1\{J_m=L\})_{j,L},
\end{equation}
are bounded martingale differences: the incoming mark resets the known
microscopic state. Cross-time covariances vanish, and the two row blocks
have disjoint indicators at a common time. At a fixed complete positive
generator the bounded martingale central limit theorem and ergodicity give
\begin{gather}
 \sqrt n(\widehat\mu-\mu)\ \Rightarrow\ N(0,\Omega),\qquad
 \Omega=\operatorname{blockdiag}(C_I/p_I),\label{eq:moment-clt}\\
 p_+=\frac{\pi_xr}{\pi_xr+\pi_ys},\qquad p_-=1-p_+,\\
 (C_I)_{jL,\ell M}
 =\mathbf1\{L=M\}\widehat\Psi_{IL}(\lambda_j+\lambda_\ell)
  -\widehat\Psi_{IL}(\lambda_j)\widehat\Psi_{IM}(\lambda_\ell).
 \label{eq:moment-covariance}
\end{gather}
Replacing $C_I/(np_I)$ by the empirical within-row feature covariance divided
by $n_I$ gives a consistent estimate $\widehat C$ of the moment covariance.
This argument does not assume independent successive marks or independence
conditional on the random row counts.

For a consistent interior fit, let
$A=(J_{\log}^T J_{\log})^{-1}J_{\log}^T$ and
$g_{\log}=\operatorname{diag}(\theta)\nabla_\theta\sig$. The estimated
entropy variance is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat V_\sig=g_{\log}^T A\widehat C A^Tg_{\log}.
 \label{eq:entropy-delta}
\end{equation}
An SVD implementation avoids forming the squared-condition normal equations.
The interval $\widehat\sig\pm1.96\sqrt{\widehat V_\sig}$, intersected with
$[0,\infty)$, has only pointwise asymptotic justification at an interior
nonequilibrium truth. At equilibrium the first entropy derivative vanishes;
near a sparse boundary the approximation need not be accurate. A global
moment minimizer on a fixed compact positive rate box is consistent by exact
injectivity and uniform criterion convergence. Our multistart numerical
optimizer is not a certificate of global minimization.

For an explicit implementation we impose the numerical rate box
$[10^{-14},20]$, use $\lambda=(0.2,1,5)$, three fixed starting points,
analytic derivatives, and stopping tolerances $10^{-10}$. The box is a stated
computational restriction, not physical evidence for a reverse-rate floor.
We flag estimates at its boundary or with unresolved logarithmic rank.
A separate practical warning suppresses a suggested interval if either
same-mark count is below 20; this threshold is a heuristic, not a coverage
theorem. It addresses a concrete failure: zero observed $++$ events give
zero empirical variance for those moment features despite positive true
variance when $b>0$.

We simulated 100 independent stationary CTMC trajectories per model and
sample size, for $n=500,5000,20000$. Only resolved marks and their times enter
the fit. The interior model is $(1.2,0.8,0.9,0.4,0.3,1.1)$, with
$\sig=0.20195$; the two sparse examples are $Q(0.2,0.01)$ and
$Q(0.2,e^{-25})$, with entropies $0.13143$ and $1.17303$. All true rates
exceed the numerical lower bound. Table~\ref{tab:finite-data} reports all
point estimates, including boundary fits, and the raw delta calibration
before the additional same-mark warning. Missing intervals are not counted
as successful coverage.

At the interior truth, 5000 records give 8.8\% relative entropy RMSE; 20000
give 4.6\% and a median interval width of $0.0343$. The oracle asymptotic
calculation predicts roughly $1.44\times10^4$ records for a 95\% half-width
of 10\% of this entropy, which is a planning estimate rather than a
finite-sample guarantee. At $b=0.01$, 20000 records give 6.0\% relative RMSE.
All 900 selected optimizations converged, but this did not imply reliable
entropy inference. For $b=e^{-25}$, 45--50\% of fits reach the lower floor
and lose numerical rank; the remaining raw intervals have only 7--16\%
conditional coverage. Every rare-case trajectory has zero microscopic
$h\to x$ jumps and zero observed $++$ pairs. The former is a simulation
diagnostic unavailable to inference; the latter suppresses all suggested
intervals under the practical count warning. At 20000 records the median
maximum fitted moment residual is only $5.8\times10^{-4}$, yet the entropy
RMSE remains $0.64$. Small observable residuals do not establish accurate
rare-rate or entropy estimates.

This pipeline demonstrates useful inference in regular examples and exposes
its failure when a reverse rate is unresolved. It does not establish
uniform finite-sample coverage, an efficient estimator, or an optimal
sample-complexity exponent. Weighted moments or a full marked-trajectory
likelihood can change efficiency, while honest upper uncertainty near the
singular boundary still requires explicit additional information.
