# Quantitative rare-rate test

Mathematical status: conventionally proved; exact identities and independent 100-digit stationary/flux checks support the derivation. Inputs are the preserved three-state matrix in manuscript Eq. `treefamily`. No external numerical dataset, random seed, Poisson approximation or rate clipping is used.

For fixed p>1, compare Q(e,k) and Q(e,k^p), k=exp(-1/e²), with stationary starts, continuous full trajectories over H, and e tending to zero. Stationary TV is at most |k-k^p|: the quotient-rule bound on its derivative is (3-2k)/Z <=1 on 0<=k<=e<1/2. Per-row excess-clock hazard is at most k-k^p. Therefore full microscopic path TV <= (H+1)(k-k^p); data processing gives the same bound for observed edge records. Sum of test errors <=gamma<1 requires H>=(1-gamma)/(k-k^p)-1.

The entropy ratio tends to p. The log horizon lower bound divided by the smaller entropy squared tends to 9, or by the larger entropy squared to 9/p². For any smaller positive exponent this implies an eventual exponential lower bound. It does not assert a matching achievable rate for coarse-data estimators. The often tempting multiplicative approximation k~exp(-9 sigma²) is not justified by sigma~1/(3e); the correct equivalence is logarithmic.

Falsification/counterexplanation: fixing e across the alternatives removes the much easier order-e difference from the limiting tree. If the one-parameter relation between e and k were known a priori for all competitors, these alternatives would not be admissible, so this lower bound would not apply. Uniform upper-confidence impossibility over the unrestricted class is separately proved in the manuscript and is stronger than a merely large finite necessary horizon.

Direct reverse-jump detection has the elementary necessary bound H >= (1-beta)/(pi_h k) from its expected count and Markov's inequality. It is not exactly a Poisson count. As e decreases, pi_h tends to 1/3 and resolved-event intensity tends to 2/3. The same exponential order therefore applies to expected observed-event counts for stationary fixed-horizon records; no fixed-count experiment theorem is inferred automatically.

Reproduction: `python -B analysis/revision/rare_event.py`. Output: [rare-event.json](../../outputs/revision/rare-event.json). The script checks weights, trace, cycle current and stationary derivative numerator exactly with SymPy, and compares entropy formulas to independent stationary solves and direct edge-flux entropy at 100 digits. It also independently checks the covered-vertex graph inverse algebra in a three-state case with an unobserved pair. Versions, domain, source hash, decimal inputs and tolerances are stored in the output. The conventional coupling and testing proof is in [rare-event-section.tex](rare-event-section.tex); finite numerical cases are not its proof.
