## Main assessment

Lead with the quantified distinction between generator recovery, entropy recovery, local upper ceilings and finite-data inference. Both waiting-time thermodynamic inference and positive-model equivalence have extensive antecedents. The defensible contribution is the precise local-ceiling classification for the stated resolved-edge experiment, the constructive small-state inverse and its entropy-instability consequence, and the exhaustively classified balanced five-state entropy fiber. The revised statistical/conditioning experiments give this distinction practical content; they should not be described as the first study of finite-statistics or inverse conditioning.

The strongest newly identified direct comparison is Fritz--Ertel--Seifert (2025), which explicitly studies finite timing/label resolution and finite observation statistics for the same class of resolved-transition waiting estimators. Skinner--Dunkel (2021, both papers) must be cited alongside Ehrich and Nitzan for compatible-model minimization. The closely related semi-Markov literature has a consequential time-reversal qualification, made explicit in the 2024 Bisker reply and Blom et al. study. The source of the observed path reverse must be specified rather than assuming that any waiting-time asymmetry is physical dissipation.

No inspected work supplies the manuscript's particular all-N single-pair local-ceiling theorem or whole balanced five-state physical entropy fiber. This is a **bounded negative literature finding**, not proof of novelty. The elementary full-vertex-coverage resolvent inversion added in this revision should be presented as a useful sufficient condition without a priority claim.

## Proposed introduction/limitations text

The following paragraphs can be incorporated with their scopes intact. They deliberately state distinctions rather than priority claims.

```latex
Much thermodynamic inference asks for a lower bound: the least microscopic
dissipation compatible with observed currents, coarse transitions, or
waiting times. Thermodynamic uncertainty relations constrain this least
cost from current fluctuations~\cite{BaratoSeifert2015,Gingrich2016};
semi-Markov and waiting-time methods use further temporal
information~\cite{Martinez2019,vanDerMeer2022,Harunari2022}.
Compatible-model minimization makes the lower-bound problem
explicit~\cite{SkinnerDunkel2021PNAS,SkinnerDunkel2021PRL,Ehrich2021,Nitzan2023}.
Our question concerns the upper end of the same type of inverse problem:
can observations exclude arbitrarily large microscopic entropy production?
A valid lower bound, even one that is sharp under a chosen topology, does
not answer that question over a larger model class.

Finite observation is a separate limitation. Recent work compares
waiting-time and current estimators under finite statistics, blurred
labels and temporal binning~\cite{FritzErtelSeifert2025,YuHarunari2024}.
We instead analyze reconstruction of microscopic rates and the resulting
entropy uncertainty within a specified identifiable class. The distinction
between structural identification and statistical learnability is already
well developed for hidden Markov models~\cite{Gassiat2016,Abraham2023}.
Our sparse-boundary example isolates another obstruction: the rate inverse
can remain continuous while the entropy functional becomes singular.

The observation model matters mathematically and physically. Aggregated
Markov models admit equivalent canonical representations that need not
preserve nonnegative microscopic rates~\cite{Bruno2005}; positive HMM, MAP
and phase-type realization theory provides related similarity and cone
methods~\cite{Vanluyten2008,HorvathTelek2007,HeZhang2006,HeZhang2008}.
General coarse trajectories need not have our resolved-edge reset
property. Their physical time reverse also requires care when the
coarse-graining is nonlocal in time~\cite{Blom2024,BiskerReply2024}.
Recent continuous-space estimators address observations without
microscopic reset events~\cite{FritzSeifert2026}; those experiments are
outside the finite-state inverse problem considered here.
```

## Remaining source and novelty limitations

The specific unresolved originals from the earlier realization audit remain Kienker (1989), Larget (1998), Rydén (1996), Förster--Nagy (2000), and some original invariant-polytope/orbit references. An original full Kienker source was not recovered by this expansion; Bruno's attribution is retained as secondary for Kienker's results. These gaps forbid an exhaustive priority claim. They do not supply counterevidence against the stated proofs.

Fresh inaccessible routes included PMC's original Martínez article and Bisker reply (browser check); author/journal-copy alternatives succeeded for both. The Blom institutional journal PDF and the Esposito institutional PDF failed; specified original preprints were read instead. The Hartich--Godec publisher comment was inaccessible, so this audit does not pretend to have read it: the substantive time-reversal counterexample was directly read in Blom and in the reply's explicit reconstruction. No search snippet was substituted for a theorem read.

A distinction preserved from the earlier audit: a bounded search that finds no matching statement is not proof of originality. The paper should be positioned as a rigorous resolved-transition inverse study with a clear entropy upper-bound question. PRE or JSTAT are plausible subject fits; selecting among them is an editorial judgment, not evidence of correctness. The abstract should not promise a generic five-state bifurcation unless the separate continuation/proof analysis supports that quantifier.
