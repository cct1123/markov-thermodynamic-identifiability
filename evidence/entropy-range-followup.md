# Entropy-range frontier followup

Inspected 2026-09-10 UTC; checkpoint 04:49 UTC. Bounded source check for a proposed singleton-or-unbounded entropy range theorem for a fixed bidirected four-state CTMC topology, a linearly minimal realization, and the complete marked waiting-time kernel for one individually resolved edge pair. This memo does not validate the proposed theorem. No exploratory computations were performed and no source PDFs were saved locally. Earlier realization references remain in [realization-frontier.md](realization-frontier.md).

## Assessment

**Inference from the inspected literature:** the proposed classification was not located. Its ingredients have substantial antecedents: exact observational equivalence through similarity; indistinguishable equilibrium and nonequilibrium CTMCs; equivalent models with one-way boundary edges; numerical evidence of unbounded entropy over exact four-state observational equivalence; and closed minimal realization orbits. A contribution should therefore be framed, if proved, as the exact classification for the specified observation and topology class, rather than discovery of hidden entropy ambiguity, the boundary mechanism, or realization-orbit compactness. This bounded search cannot certify novelty.

## Closest entropy-range antecedent: Ehrich (2021)

**Primary result; full preprint inspected.** Jannik Ehrich, *Tightest bound on hidden entropy production from partially observed dynamics*, J. Stat. Mech. (2021), 083214; [arXiv:2105.08803v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.08803v2). Section 4, printed pp. 13–14, Eqs. (39)–(47), parameterizes observationally equivalent four-state **discrete-time** models with two individually visible states and two states sharing the hidden symbol. The hidden count is known; topology is explicitly unknown. The construction assumes nonsingular P(2)=CB, hence invertible B and C. Two free entries determine the other blocks; all jump probabilities are preserved.

Section 4.1, p. 14, reports that finer grids yield highest entropy values growing “seemingly unbounded,” attributing this to an almost absolutely irreversible edge. This is numerical evidence with an identified mechanism, not a general boundary-existence or divergence theorem. Section 4.2 tests lower-bound quality on 50 random full matrices. Section 5, p. 18, anticipates continuous-time extensions and permits future restrictions to prescribed transitions. It does not give the fixed-topology CTMC dichotomy. Section 3.4, pp. 11–12, Eq. (38), instead proves exact entropy recovery for a prescribed unicyclic network.

**Inference:** invertibility of the two hidden coupling blocks is stronger than general linear minimality. Extending the result to all minimal four-state kernels, including singular coupling blocks, is a substantive distinction. Neither this paper's local numerical sweeps nor its unicyclic result settles the proposed classification.

## One-way CTMC boundary models: Siekmann (2026)

**Primary derivation; full publisher and author versions inspected.** Ivo Siekmann, *Modelling ion channels with a view towards identifiability*, Bull. Math. Biol. 88, 2 (2026), accepted 31 October 2025; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11538-025-01558-3), [full author PDF](https://researchonline.ljmu.ac.uk/id/eprint/27780/1/Modelling%20ion%20channels%20with%20a%20view%20towards%20identifiability.pdf).

Section 3.4 develops Kienker's three-state binary-aggregate example: closed states C1,C2 and open state O3. Equations (52)–(55), pp. 22–23, parameterize equivalent full generators by q13,q23 with four dwell-law invariants fixed. Section 3.4.3, Eq. (56), observes that q13=0 need not force q31=0. Equation (62), p. 24, explicitly constructs q12=0 with q21 nonzero and an infinite equivalent family obtained by varying q23. Section 3.4.4 and Fig. 7, pp. 25–26, describe finite positivity intervals and distinguish classes containing a nonnegative detailed-balance representative from classes containing none.

The paper does not establish an entropy-production range theorem. Its observation is the binary aggregate trajectory, with aggregate crossings unresolved; it is not a selected individually resolved microscopic edge pair. Generic distinct hidden decay rates underlie the displayed coordinate formulas.

**Inference:** finite rate intervals do not imply bounded entropy: a reverse rate can tend to zero inside such a bounded set. A fixed-support interior path to an irreducible one-way boundary would turn this mechanism into divergence, but that path, irreducibility, and the limiting flux require verification. The source's boundary parameterizations are strong prior art for the mechanism, not proof of the proposed four-state result.

## Exact equilibrium ambiguity: Wagner and Timmer (2000)

**Primary result; full author PDF inspected.** Mirko Wagner and Jens Timmer, *The Effects of Non-Identifiability on Testing for Detailed Balance in Aggregated Markov Models for Ion-Channel Gating*, Biophys. J. 79, 2918–2924 (2000); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0006-3495(00)76529-5), [full PDF](https://jeti.uni-freiburg.de/papers/detailed_balance_printed.pdf).

“Criterion for non-identifiability,” pp. 2919–2920, uses row-normalized similarity matrices block diagonal by output aggregate. With appropriate regularity, an implicit-function argument preserves structural zeros and gives local equivalent positive generators. “Testing for detailed balance,” p. 2920, shows exact equilibrium/nonequilibrium ambiguity for the four-state loop with equal open dwell times: a two-parameter equivalent family has only one detailed-balance constraint. This is distinct from the later finite-data power simulations. Kienker (1989), Fredkin–Rice (1986), and Wagner et al. (1999) are explicit antecedents. There is no entropy-range or global boundary theorem here, and its binary state aggregation does not resolve microscopic crossings.

## Closed minimal realization orbits are established theory

**Result in the authors' monograph; full proof inspected.** Uwe Helmke and John B. Moore, *Optimization and Dynamical Systems*, author-hosted second edition, March 1996; [full PDF](https://users.cecs.anu.edu.au/~john/papers/BOOK/B04.PDF). Section 7.4, Lemma 4.1 (cross-referenced elsewhere as Lemma 7.4.1), printed p. 212, states that a complex triple (A,B,C) is controllable and observable iff its similarity orbit is closed in the ambient triple space and has complex dimension n². The necessity proof identifies the orbit with the fiber of the continuous map to all Markov parameters. The real orbit is explicitly used as a closed submanifold on printed p. 288. The PDF page indices are 222 and 298, respectively, counting from zero.

Section 7.4, p. 209, attributes its development to Helmke (1993a): *Balanced Realizations for Linear Systems: A Variational Approach*, SIAM J. Control Optim. 31(1), 1–15, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1137/0331001). That article's abstract and references were accessible; its full text was not obtained. No claim about its exact theorem numbering is made.

**Inference, not a source theorem about CTMCs:** with the observed input/output blocks fixed, nonnegative generator rates and fixed trace bound every rate. Intersecting the closed minimal orbit with the closed generator inequalities and specified structural-zero conditions therefore gives a compact set. Strictly positive rates on the prescribed support select an open part whose closure is compact. This uses the trace of the full generator (equal to that of the killed generator when killing only deletes off-diagonal marked rates). The unrestricted similarity orbit itself is not claimed compact.

Closedness does not by itself prove irreducibility at the boundary, a one-way boundary's existence, or positive limiting flux. Nor does it prove that entropy must vary on every nontrivial generator family. These remain separate steps of the proposed classification. Calling this compactness argument a new general positive-realization theorem would overstate the contribution.
