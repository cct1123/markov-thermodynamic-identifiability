## Decision and falsification target

The strongest remaining candidate contribution is the explicit **whole physical entropy fiber and its sharp transition** on the balanced theta ray. General finite positive-realization fibers, similarity descriptions, semialgebraicity, invariant-polytope methods, and the distinction between canonical and physical representations are established. Small-order rational inversion and continuity on a bounded parameter class should be supporting arguments, not independent headline novelty claims.

The precise central target is the repository's [balanced theorem](../analysis/theta-balanced-boundary.md): irreducible simple bidirected row CTMCs, even states, one channel per ordered pair, known endpoints of one resolved observed pair, exact joint next-mark/time laws, unknown competitor topology, and at most five states. The full kernel has linear minimal order five. At the specified positive quartic root there are exactly two generators modulo hidden labels and two distinct finite stationary entropy rates; the subcritical fiber is unbounded and the supercritical generator is unique. A source result would preempt this claim if its hypotheses already implied this physical whole-fiber cardinality and entropy separation, or if a stated construction specialized to it without an additional global support-exhaustion argument. Mere production of two equivalent models, two canonical forms, finitely many topologies, or two Coxian representations does not meet that test.

For the [fixed-trace neighborhood theorem](../analysis/entropy-neighborhood-audit.md), the corresponding target is: at **every** source with two fully hidden states, every positive observed-law TV neighborhood contains unbounded finite entropy at the same state count, observed rates and exact trace, under a common finite upper rate bound. A precedent with those quantifiers would substantially preempt it. Unbounded rates, added hidden states, a numerical boundary maximum, or generic nonidentifiability alone are weaker. The general statistical impossibility deduction is already prior art; this memo does not claim a new confidence-set theorem.

## New full-text comparisons

Each entry gives the version actually inspected. “Full text available” means access beyond an abstract, not a claim to have independently reproduced every proof. Source-derived descriptions are deliberately short; no long source excerpts are reproduced.

### 1. Complete equivalent positive HMM sets: closes an earlier access gap

B. Vanluyten, J. C. Willems, B. De Moor, *Equivalence of state representations for hidden Markov models*, Systems & Control Letters **57**, 410–419 (2008), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sysconle.2007.10.004), [full author-hosted journal PDF](https://homes.esat.kuleuven.be/~sistawww/smc/jwillems/Articles/JournalArticles/2008.3.pdf). Read Sections 2–5 and Appendix, including definitions and proof passages. Proposition 2, p. 414, characterizes all same-order positive Mealy realizations when minimal as a **quasi-Mealy** model: a common invertible similarity, normalization `T1=1`, and transformed transition/initial nonnegativity. Theorem 2 gives semialgebraicity; Appendix p. 419 supplies the projection/quantifier-elimination argument. Proposition 3 treats positive order exceeding quasi order. Theorem 4, p. 415, requires distinct state output distributions and full-rank transition for its Moore permutation-uniqueness conclusion.

**Inference:** this directly preempts novelty in describing an entire equivalent positive-model set by similarity plus inequalities. It neither counts the endpoint's physical set nor preserves its prescribed resolved edges automatically. The Moore theorem's hypotheses must not be imported into an aggregation with repeated emissions. The paper's semialgebraic conclusion is a framework, not a computed cardinality.

### 2. Continuous-time MAP equivalence

G. Horváth and M. Telek, *A minimal representation of Markov arrival processes and a moments matching method*, Performance Evaluation **64**, 1153–1168 (2007), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.peva.2007.06.001), [full author-uploaded preprint](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/222578381_A_minimal_representation_of_Markov_arrival_processes_and_a_moments_matching_method), dated 6 June 2007. Read Sections 2–3 and 6–7. Theorem 3, preprint p. 8, gives normalized simultaneous similarity for two nonredundant irreducible MAP representations with equal stated transform; Theorem 4, p. 9, bounds independent parameters. The proof explicitly uses the marginal representation's relevant modes through its initial and terminal vectors. Sections 6–7 construct and numerically seek a Markovian representation.

**Inference:** normalized continuous-time equivalence and moment parameterization are prior art. Their nonredundancy hypothesis must not be replaced by a different marked-system minimality notion without proof. Algorithm failure, especially on sparse examples discussed in Section 7, does not establish infeasibility. No entropy count or inverse-stability theorem is supplied. The original-source access gap in earlier notes is closed by this preprint read, not by treating its numerical search as a decision procedure.

### 3. The two-state MAP continuum

P. Ramírez-Cobo, R. E. Lillo, M. P. Wiper, *Nonidentifiability of the Two-State Markovian Arrival Process*, Journal of Applied Probability **47**, 630–649 (2010), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1239/jap/1285335400), [full journal PDF](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/874FEE16A7778E68774F3B6FC5BAF3F1/S0021900200006975a.pdf/nonidentifiability_of_the_twostate_markovian_arrival_process.pdf). Read model definitions pp. 631–633, Propositions 4.1–4.4, Theorems 4.1–4.2 pp. 637–639, discussion p. 641, and Appendix D. They construct a parameter family with identical joint interarrival laws, with explicit denominator and positivity restrictions.

**Inference:** continuous arrival-law ambiguity is established even at two internal phases. This is not a contradiction of cap-three physical recovery: `D1` permits self-arrivals and several transitions with the same arrival mark, and `D0` may contain unobserved transitions between the same phases. The repository instead fixes two rank-one physical event maps with known resets and forbids parallel channels. Do not extend the displayed positive-parameter family to every zero stratum without checking its inequalities, or confuse MAP phase count with a resolved CTMC's hidden-state count.

### 4. Marking arrivals does not generally remove ambiguity

J. Rodríguez, R. E. Lillo, P. Ramírez-Cobo, *On the identifiability of the two-state BMAP*, [May 2012 working-paper full text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/254401538_On_the_identifiability_of_the_two-state_BMAP), [institutional metadata](https://e-archivo.uc3m.es/entities/publication/b33f514d-5259-4e78-844c-c04ddb67f534). Read Section 2, Definition 1/Eq. (7) p. 6, Proposition 2 and Theorem 1 pp. 12–13, and proof discussion. Their equality includes all joint waiting times **and batch-size marks**, yet a continuous equivalent family survives. The event matrices are general nonnegative matrices, not prescribed edge resets. Proposition 1 omits approximately 43 pages of rational expressions; the all-length argument refers back to the MAP proof. Those calculations were not independently reproduced here.

The journal successor, *Nonidentifiability of the Two-State BMAP*, **18**, 81–106 (2016), online 23 April 2014, [DOI/primary abstract](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11009-014-9401-z), extends the abstract's claim to all maximum batch sizes at least two. **Access:** journal abstract/references only; do not attribute its full proof to the earlier preprint. This strengthens the warning against claiming generic marked identifiability, not a finite physical entropy count.

### 5. Canonical two-phase reconstruction

L. Bodrog, A. Heindl, G. Horváth, M. Telek, *A Markovian canonical form of second-order matrix-exponential processes*, European Journal of Operational Research **190**, 459–477 (2008), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejor.2007.06.020), [full author-uploaded preprint](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/4755643_A_Markovian_canonical_form_of_second-order_matrix-exponential_processes). Read Definition 1 pp. 8–9, Theorem 3 p. 13, Section 6 pp. 14–16 and associated restrictions. Two canonical forms distinguish correlation regimes; stationary second-order matrix-exponential laws have Markovian/acyclic MAP representations. Reconstruction uses three interarrival moments and lag-one correlation.

**Inference:** low-order explicit inversion is mature prior art. Canonical uniqueness chooses a representative from a physical equivalence class; it does not make every equivalent generator equal or preserve thermodynamic entropy. The repository's six physical rates are recovered because the observable reset geometry is stronger, not because generic two-phase arrival models are identifiable. Positivity/rank and degenerate-parameter restrictions matter for any stability comparison. The uploaded preprint's version date was not verified; journal metadata is the bibliographic date.

### 6. Numerical likelihood difficulties are a different assertion

E. Carrizosa and P. Ramírez-Cobo, *Maximum likelihood estimation in the two-state Markovian arrival process*, [arXiv:1401.3105v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.3105v1), submitted 14 January 2014; [full author-uploaded preprint](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/259718184_Maximum_likelihood_estimation_in_the_two-state_Markovian_arrival_process), cover dated 15 January. Read Sections 2–4 and references. Canonical coordinates address redundant parameterizations; Section 4.2 pp. 10–11 treats underflow in products of waiting-time matrices and proposes scaling. Their optimization also encounters multiple extrema.

**Inference:** these are numerical/estimation issues, not a theorem that the population inverse is discontinuous, and not unbounded thermodynamic entropy. In particular, the repository's tree sequence is not explained by computer underflow. Treating the paper as an “ill-conditioning theorem” would overstate it.

### 7. Dynamic invariant triangles are also prior art

Q.-M. He and H. Zhang, *PH-Invariant Polytopes and Coxian Representations of Phase Type Distributions*, Stochastic Models **22**, 383–409 (2006), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/15326340600820349), [full journal PDF](https://www.engineering.uwaterloo.ca/~q7he/Z_PDF_PS_Published/2006_SM_PH_Polytope_HE_Zhang.pdf). Read definitions, Example 3.2 pp. 389–391, Theorem 4.1 pp. 397–398 and its notes, and Example 6.1 pp. 407–408. They construct invariant polytopes corresponding to ordered Coxian representations, including real Jordan blocks and a three-mode triangle example. Their maximality discussion concerns the constructed ordered family.

**Inference:** replacing a static nested simplex with a dynamical invariant triangle is not by itself novel. Their scalar PH entrance/absorption problem does not exhaust all three-ray cones satisfying the repository's two-port containment, output and reciprocal-support restrictions, or count stationary entropy values. The relevant extra work is the project's sharp global envelope and equality case, plus separate disconnected-support exhaustion. No numerical counterexample search is needed to establish this hypothesis mismatch.

### 8. Forward citation: Coxian order need not equal pole count

Q.-M. He and H. Zhang, *An Algorithm for Computing Minimal Coxian Representations*, INFORMS Journal on Computing **20**, 179–190 (2008), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1287/ijoc.1070.0228), [full journal PDF](https://www.engineering.uwaterloo.ca/~q7he/Z_PDF_PS_Published/2008_JOC_Reprint_HE_Zhang.pdf). Read Section 5's algorithm and Section 6 Example 1, pp. 186–188, plus references. The search concerns minimal **ordered Coxian** representations for real-pole PH distributions; the example has three poles but arbitrarily large triangular order. It explicitly builds on the 2006 invariant-polytope paper.

**Inference:** a canonical positive order, unrestricted positive order and linear minimal order are different quantities. This source prevents a shortcut from a three-pole scalar representation to the theta problem's cap five. It supplies no finite unrestricted physical-fiber count.

### 9. Quantitative HMM inverse frontiers

K. Abraham, E. Gassiat, Z. The tree example's continuous generator inverse but singular entropy is a different obstruction, not a refutation or rediscovery of their inverse frontier.

## Mathematical distinctions that change positioning

For the repository, write `D0=T`, `D+=q_xy e_x e_y^T`, `D-=q_yx e_y e_x^T`. This embeds the process in marked-arrival notation, but the two event matrices are prescribed rank-one physical maps. Their post-event states are known; the next-mark/time rows therefore compose into the full sequential observed law. A generic MAP/BMAP event can leave a distribution over internal phases, so its full law involves matrix products that do not reset in this way. Borrowing a general marked-arrival theorem requires this restriction to be imposed, not dropped.

The cap-five theorem's hard step is not normalized similarity itself. It is positivity and bidirectional support across **every** hidden support stratum, including reducible hidden blocks, at the equality case of a sharp invariant-cone bound. Counting only interior similarities, only a canonical topology, or only a boundary retract would miss the relevant quantifier. Conversely, a finite static fiber already prevents advertising finite alternatives as an unexpected possibility in positive realization theory generally.

For the cap-three neighborhood result, bounded rates permit subsequential generator limits. Exact reset-kernel recovery identifies the limit, giving continuity of the generator inverse, including trees. At a positive triangle, stationary entropy is then continuous. At a paired-zero edge, `flux difference * log(flux ratio)` can diverge even when both rates converge to zero. This is visible under complete generator observation too. Fixed trace does not bound rate ratios. These are deductions from the repository's proofs, not claims derived from the HMM sources. The potentially publishable strengthening is the exact dimensional/topological classification and quantified fixed-trace observation neighborhoods, with the general hidden-pair theorem doing more than this elementary three-state limit.

### Sharpened statements inspected after the initial memo

Read [publication-stability-audit.md](../analysis/publication-stability-audit.md), Sections 3–5, on the coordinator's follow-up. Its three-positive-argument Laplace inverse removes the prior rate cap for joint row laws, even with unknown observed rates, at every genuinely irreducible three-state source. The selected row has a positive hidden entrance; its Schur-complement row sum has the form `u+v/(lambda+h)`, `v>0`. Three values recover the pole and coefficients through nonzero source denominators. Thus recovery is locally Lipschitz in row TV and continuous under weak row-law convergence. The older compactness argument above remains sufficient under a cap; it is no longer the strongest result. This worker read the new algebra/proof and its executable-check specification, but did not independently rerun that checker.

**Closest-result difference:** entries 2 and 5 already provide finite transform/moment reconstruction of canonical arrival models; entry 9 provides explicit inverse-conditioning bounds. None of their read statements supplies this physical three-state reset inverse or its cap-free weak-law conclusion. The new inverse is nonetheless a short rational interpolation argument once the Schur complement is written. It strengthens the technical result and avoids an unjustified differentiation-continuity shortcut, but is a supporting lemma rather than a persuasive standalone novelty claim. Its condition number is source-dependent; a two-state source under cap three lacks the positive entrance and is excluded. The cap-free claim has not been proved for a single fixed stationary observation window.

The resulting arbitrary-fixed-N criterion says that some positive row-TV neighborhood has finite entropy supremum **iff** `N=2` or `N=3` with a complete triangle. It holds both in the unrestricted finite-rate class and after fixing the source's observed rates and exact trace. A prescribed tighter individual rate cap can invalidate it: the complete four-state unit-rate source, fixed trace minus twelve and cap one, leaves only itself. The constructive cap in the hidden-pair theorem is sufficient, not arbitrary. This exact quantifier combination, rather than the elementary missing-edge KL singularity, is the useful second candidate theorem. A reverse-rate floor, known sparse topology, extra resolved edges, or state counts allowed to vary is a different class.
