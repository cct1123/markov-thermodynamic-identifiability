\subsection{Relation to inference and realization literature}
Much thermodynamic inference asks for a lower bound: the least microscopic
dissipation compatible with observed currents, coarse transitions, or
waiting times. Thermodynamic uncertainty relations constrain this least
cost from current fluctuations~\cite{BaratoSeifert2015,Gingrich2016};
semi-Markov and waiting-time methods use further temporal
information~\cite{Martinez2019,vanDerMeer2022,Harunari2022}.
Compatible-model minimization makes the lower-bound problem
explicit~\cite{SkinnerDunkel2021PNAS,SkinnerDunkel2021PRL,Ehrich2021,Nitzan2023}.
Our question concerns the upper end of the same type of inverse problem:
can observations exclude arbitrarily large microscopic entropy production?
A valid lower bound, even one that is sharp under a chosen topology, does
not answer that question over a larger model class.

Finite observation is a separate limitation. Recent work compares
waiting-time and current estimators under finite statistics, blurred
labels and temporal binning~\cite{FritzErtelSeifert2025}. Spatial lumping
and blurred transitions reveal additional resolution-dependent
dissipative scales~\cite{YuHarunari2024}.
We instead analyze reconstruction of microscopic rates and the resulting
entropy uncertainty within a specified identifiable class. The distinction
between structural identification and statistical learnability is already
well developed for hidden Markov models~\cite{Gassiat2016,Abraham2023}.
Our sparse-boundary example isolates another obstruction: the rate inverse
can remain continuous while the entropy functional becomes singular.

The observation model matters mathematically and physically. Aggregated
Markov models admit equivalent canonical representations that need not
preserve nonnegative microscopic rates~\cite{Bruno2005}; positive HMM, MAP
and phase-type realization theory provides related similarity and cone
methods~\cite{Vanluyten2008,HorvathTelek2007,HeZhang2006,HeZhang2008}.
General coarse trajectories need not have our resolved-edge reset
property. Their physical time reverse also requires care when the
coarse-graining is nonlocal in time~\cite{Blom2024,BiskerReply2024}.
Recent continuous-space estimators address observations without
microscopic reset events~\cite{FritzSeifert2026}; those experiments are
outside the finite-state inverse problem considered here.

Hidden entropy and its fluctuation relations also depend on the microscopic time-reversal convention~\cite{KawaguchiNakayama2013}; they do not provide an upper ceiling from our partial waiting laws.
